## Construction warmup

`capabilities/economy/startup/self-build.ts` projects one supplied uninterrupted at-station worker interval, bounded to 1,500 ticks. It calls the existing `minerStep` action selector with local projected effects. Construction therefore takes priority whenever the miner carries energy, exactly as execution does. Inputs supply actual remaining progress, cargo/capacity, effective harvest/build power, source stock/capacity, regeneration period/phase and available work ticks. A clipped horizon returns incomplete progress; it does not invent replacement workers. Travel, spawning, retirement, unsafe periods, traffic, source-capacity changes, powers, boosts, repairs, competing miners and alternative builders require separate facts/schedules or remeasurement before this projection can be used as a runtime guarantee.

Unboosted construction consumes one energy per progress. Engine4.3.2 sources start a regeneration clock when stock becomes partial and refill after the action frame with timer one. The local engine implementation and existing archived trace support this ordering. The forecast reports harvested, constructed and regenerated energy, cargo, source stock, action/idle ticks and remaining progress. Conservation checks distinguish source refill from new harvest and avoid treating carried energy as new funding. An already due timer remains representable as zero.

The 4-WORK source miner harvests8 energy, then builds8 on the next tick even with CARRY50. The archived bootstrap starts that cycle at35; after1,250 actions the container is observed complete at1,285. Forecast checkpoints reproduce site progress, cargo and actual source stock through all four regeneration boundaries, including final stock1,300. This calibrates the current duty policy rather than assuming full-CARRY batches or continuous production while building.

## Cash, replacement and recovery

`startup/cash.ts` evaluates explicit finite flows for births, home-funded construction, upkeep and confirmed/projected deliveries. Recurrence counts are finite, IDs unique, at most128 schedules and a10,000-tick inclusive horizon. Schedules can represent each idle courier and each replacement birth at its own admission time. This layer does not choose spawn timing, infer travel readiness, create income or reserve anything. A caller must supply achievable schedules and enough worker/recruitment/dispatch facts before using a projection for admission.

Costs occur before receipts in a frame: incoming effects cannot finance an earlier native spawn admission. Minimum bank balance determines required home capital and any shortfall against a protected reserve. Initial bank is excluded from cash surplus and recovery income. Home-funded construction is a real cash debit; construction supplied from remote harvest is instead an explicit recovery target and is never subtracted from initial home cash. `constructionRecoveryMargin` is the delivered cash surplus after covering that target. This is a conservative construction-recovery benchmark, not a second physical expense or an assertion that self-harvested energy was taken from home.

`firstRecoveryAt` is the first threshold crossing after declared readiness and actual positive deliveries. It is not a promise of sustained profit: later replacement costs may reduce the margin. Selection policy must inspect the declared horizon, final margin, funding shortfall, unpriced future costs and startup facts rather than accepting that timestamp alone. Finite schedules avoid silently double-charging initial births through both one-off capital and recurring amortized body rates.

## Finite spawn occupancy and cash schedules

`startup/spawns.ts` now turns explicit finite native-shaped requests and supplied spawn availability into birth cash flows. It reuses `allocateSpawns` for priority, room matching, exclusive expiry, live-name exclusion and wait/skip behavior; successful projected births occupy their chosen spawn for body-part count times three ticks. Slots and requests are copied, with no persistent queue writes, native effects or admission reservations. Bounds are 64 requests, 16 spawns, 128 combined flows and a 10,000-tick inclusive horizon.

External home expenses precede allocation; same-frame income is credited afterwards and can fund a birth only from the next frame. Each projected birth is charged exactly once in the shared cash forecast. Protected cash limits each batch, while external expenses may still create a reported shortfall. A birth admitted at the final horizon frame can become active later; `activeAt` explicitly retains that date. Unscheduled requests remain visible as live-name exclusions, expired windows or unmet horizon requests. These are terminal categories, not an assertion that cash was the only constraint.

This establishes spawn-occupancy and cash feasibility under supplied assumptions, not a native achievable full-project schedule. The following funding refinement explicitly models room capacity, accessible stock and supplied redistribution dates; the caller must still establish those inputs, future spawn survival/availability, request readiness and future delivery causality. All requests affecting the forecast must be supplied; omitted local/emergency work is not priced. No worker replacement, travel, construction interval or future delivery is invented. Candidate selection and runtime staging do not yet call this schedule layer. Its explicit dates/unscheduled outcomes are the next input boundary for those integrations, rather than claiming that arbitrary recurring births can all happen at once.

## Spawn-accessible funding

The schedule input now requires `funding.capacity`, `funding.initialEnergy` and finite `funding.refills`. Initial spawn/extension energy is a subset of total `cash.homeEnergy`, not an additional bank. A home with 10,000 stored energy and zero accessible spawn energy cannot launch a worker until a supplied refill settles. Body costs larger than room capacity remain unscheduled even with abundant home stock. Parallel spawns share one accessible room pool and the same protected total bank.

Refills are internal moves, never income or expense. For each frame: conservative external spending, birth admission/spending, internal refill settlement, then external income. A refill is limited by requested amount, free spawn capacity and non-spawn bank stock. Its energy can finance admission from the next frame. Same-frame remote credit cannot retroactively fund an earlier transfer; scheduled refills that lack stock/capacity report `unfilledEnergy`, without silent debt or invented energy. The result also reports actual total refilled energy and ending accessible stock. Refills beyond the declared horizon are not executed. At most64 finite refill schedules are allowed; cash-flow and horizon validation is shared with the existing cash forecast.

When external spending does not identify a physical stock pool, the forecast conservatively removes accessible stock first. This may defer a possible birth; a future pool-aware caller must not interpret it as native exact behavior. Deliveries enter the non-spawn bank until explicitly redistributed. The model does not plan distributor trips, prove future refill availability, price omitted worker commitments or reserve runtime stock. Those remain integration requirements, as do multi-wave construction/travel schedules and candidate payback policy. No production caller uses the portable spawn forecast yet; current eager/staged runtime behavior is unchanged.

## Construction from scheduled worker waves

`startup/construction.ts` composes the funded spawn forecast with the calibrated self-build duty policy. Callers identify which finite spawn requests are source builders and provide quoted outbound travel/retirement time. Only requests that actually receive a projected birth supply labor. Body WORK/CARRY determine unboosted harvest/build power and capacity. Each worker arrives at `activeAt + travelTicks` and stops at `activeAt + 1500 - retireTicks`, clipped to the inclusive action horizon. Work intervals are half-open; completed effects are observable at `startAt + actions`, so an effect issued on the final horizon action can become visible one frame later.

Unfinished progress and the source stock/regeneration phase survive between non-overlapping worker intervals. Idle gaps advance regeneration without harvesting, deliveries or construction. Newborns start with no cargo; unused outgoing worker cargo is reported separately rather than transferred into the next body or treated as income. `projectedThrough` dates the final source/cargo projection; it does not silently advance those facts through the rest of the financial horizon. Already completed work reports completion at zero. Unfunded, expired, beyond-horizon or service-exhausted births provide no construction. Empty labor schedules still validate source/progress facts.

The input contract deliberately refuses overlapping construction intervals until a contention/handoff model exists. It supports standard unboosted WORK/CARRY/MOVE source miners only; powers, boosts, damage, alternate builders, repairs, traffic, source capacity changes and unsupported lifetimes require another strategy. The tests use short synthetic service intervals to expose replacement boundaries; they are not native full-life qualification. Supplied future cash/refill schedules remain assumptions: construction does not generate home receipts or price worker travel/distribution automatically. This layer is not yet a runtime candidate-selection policy.

## Finite source delivery and project cash

`startup/delivery.ts` now composes funded births, finite construction waves, actual miner action selection and finite courier trips. After the container is ready, mining uses `minerStep` for harvest/full-CARRY/depleted-source handoff. The shared bounded source-regeneration helper advances during work and idle frames. Couriers share finite container stock; collection cannot consume a miner handoff issued later in the same frame. A full container backpressures the miner without deleting carried energy. Successor miners start empty; outgoing cargo remains separately accounted.

Only funded CARRY/MOVE couriers supply trips. Quoted outbound/loaded-return travel, minimum batches, retirement and finite standard lifetimes bound collection and return. Explicit collection/delivery delays default to one action frame each and can include measured task/receipt overhead. Credits have dated receipt events after unload; beyond-horizon credits remain uncounted cargo. First delivery and all receipts are ordered by observation date. Construction and post-build source/cargo facts retain separate projection-through dates.

The cash forecast accepts bounded, uniquely identified finite receipt events alongside the existing compact recurring flows, avoiding fictional periodic income for irregular trips. Event and recurring-flow identities cannot collide. At most160,016 events are admitted (16 courier candidates times10,001 frames), with checked dates/energy and aggregation by frame. Costs still precede same-frame receipts. The delivery result's top-level `cash` reconciles every projected birth and generated receipt, with recovery held until construction is visible; `spawning.cash` retains the original birth-admission funding assumptions. Generated income does not retroactively finance the already computed birth schedule. Input external income must remain independently justified and must not duplicate these source receipts.

The portable source model is deliberately separate from native authority. It assumes quoted safe uninterrupted travel, an accepting home sink, constant observed source capacity, and a surviving maintained container. It does not yet price container decay/repair, home sink saturation, traffic/visibility/lease changes, reservation-induced capacity transitions, boosts, damage or worker failure. Worker intervals still refuse overlapping builders until handoff is modeled. Native task timing and operating envelopes remain unqualified for this new forecast. Candidate selection and ordinary runtime do not yet call it.
