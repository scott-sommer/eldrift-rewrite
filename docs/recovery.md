# Energy recovery (ED-22)

## Recovery assessment and worker investment policy

Recovery now has plain observed room/donor facts, pure hysteresis/donor policy, a feature-owned versioned state per room, a native observation adapter and an injected application evaluator. The work feature evaluates recovery before its own population and task decisions, so this behavior does not depend on scheduler ID order. The initial assessment milestone connected workers; the emergency-economy milestone below additionally connects mining and logistics.

recoveryEnabled defaults false. Configurable defaults: recoveryInterval250, recoveryEnterEnergy10000, recoveryExitEnergy50000, recoveryDonorMinimumEnergy75000, recoveryDonorMaximumDistance4 and recoverySupportBatch25000. Combined configuration validates entry<exit. The pure policy additionally exposes minimumLevel5. Stored energy means owned storage+terminal energy, matching the legacy recovery module; it excludes carried/container/spawn energy. Entry and donor stock tests are strict; exit occurs at>=50000. Owned room eligibility is RCL>=5.

Each snapshot persists active, checkedAt, observed energy, policy fingerprint and sorted support proposals. Initial evaluation and configuration changes happen immediately (a deliberate improvement over waiting for Game.time modulo250); later assessments use the persisted interval. Disabling recovery or losing eligibility clears active state/proposals immediately when evaluated. Donor observations are lazy and occur only when an eligible assessment is due. Snapshot reconstruction retains cadence and hysteresis. Candidate donors exclude self/unowned/out-of-range/insufficient-energy rooms and sort by distance, descending stock and name. The persisted proposal list is bounded to the first100donors. Proposals are intentions, not stock reservations, deliveries or receipts. Loss-of-visibility alone does not retire stored room state; future consumers must evaluate observed eligibility before acting.

Active recovery suppresses ordinary work-selector upgrade fallback and dedicated upgrader population demand, cancelling queued upgrader requests. Downgrade-emergency selection remains enabled. Builders/fortifiers retain their other work. The initial assessment milestone did not maintain emergency miner/courier floors. Those are added below; distributor reassignment, donor support and the12500distributor restart threshold remain open.

## Emergency economy integration

Recovery evaluation is injected into mining, logistics and work, each before that feature's operational choices. Persistent assessment cadence makes repeated calls consistent without relying on scheduler order. `recoveryEconomyEnabled` defaults true but only applies while recovery is active; recoveryEnabled remains false by default. Disabling the economy option retains assessment and worker investment suppression without automatically enabling mining/hauling. The assessment-only native fixture sets this explicitly.

The pure recoveryEconomy policy derives operational values without changing operator configuration or its provenance. Active recovery enables energy mining, logistics, consumer requests, lab/link energy reclamation, source-container banking and courier spawning; it suppresses mineral mining/transport and store balancing. Other configured behavior remains explicit. Emergency body caps are configurable: recoveryMiningBodyBudget200 and recoveryCourierBodyBudget100, each additionally limited by its ordinary configured body budget. This lets a first200cost miner leave100of the initial spawn300 for a courier. Normal population reconciliation still determines station/route capacity and excludes duplicate live/spawning/pending commitments; recovery adds a minimum of one courier body's capacity even before transport requests exist.

Mining/hauling requests and actions remain in their existing implementations and ownership scopes. Exit restores normal configured behavior and cancels emergency-only pending spawn demand. Existing creeps are retained. If normal logistics is disabled, prior-tick hauling/link effects are now settled before the execution-disable guard, preserving actual delivery receipts. Disabled execution does not issue new actions; carried cargo and task state remain available for later reactivation, and observed transfer settlement releases its quantity scope. General paused-task retention/pruning remains a lifecycle/soak concern rather than being described as completed cleanup.

## Energy scavenging and admission policy

Active recovery now enables shared preservation with `preservationEnergyOnly:true`; the ordinary setting defaults false and continues preserving all resource types. Only energy drops/remains generate new recovery preservation requests. Consumer demand is still generated first, with its source commitments counted before preservation. This reuses existing source reconciliation, capacity reservations, collection/transfer tasks and confirmed delivery accounting rather than adding a scavenger executor.

An optional injected predicate in `selectHaulingTask` gates new admission before observing or reserving requests. Active recovery accepts energy requests only, including when more urgent mineral work was already queued by another owner. That other owner's request remains intact and can resume under ordinary admission. Existing admitted cargo/tasks are allowed to finish: the policy does not discard resources or claim to preempt in-flight mineral work. The generic predicate defaults to accepting all requests, preserving other callers' behavior.

## Recovery scavenger population

`scavenger-plan` ports the affordable-first/full-size policy from spawner.js:551 using the existing roadScavenger recipe. With no live or pending scavenger it snapshots the largest body affordable from current room energy, bounded by room capacity and recoveryScavengerBodyBudget (default1600). Initial priority-1 precedes energy miners at0. An undersized live scavenger permits one full-capacity body at priority1 after miners. A full-size live body or any pending/spawning scavenger suppresses duplicates. Full-size compares carry parts, move parts and total parts as in the source. The shared dispatcher still skips unaffordable requests, so priority applies among affordable work; no global spawn blocking was introduced.

`scavenger-demand` owns the distinct logistics:<room>:scavenger spawn scope. It retains queued body/identity across changing available energy, removes observed/expired/impossible requests, and cancels only that scope when recovery/scavenger spawning is disabled. Loss of room capacity or a lower configured cap re-plans an impossible pending body. No new state is allocated in inactive rooms without a spawn queue. recoveryScavengerEnabled defaults true, gated by the active recovery economy. This is separate from the ordinary courier floor; it does not relabel couriers as scavengers merely to satisfy population counts.

The native adapter observes only scoped scavengers while enabled. Both identities use the same hauling admission/task/action/reservation pipeline, so there is no duplicate scavenger executor. Courier-only demand cancellation leaves scavenger requests intact; disabling the entire hauling feature cancels both populations. Existing scavengers remain available to normal logistics after recovery exits. They use normal logistics priorities then, an explicit consolidation rather than permanent duplicated legacy role loops. As in the source, this policy reacts to loss of full-size units rather than proactively scheduling their TTL replacement; ordinary courier route replacement remains separate.
