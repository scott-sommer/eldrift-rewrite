# Household labor projection

ED-29 needs paid home workers and their actual energy use in startup economics. The existing outside-spawn cash tag describes a declared bill; it cannot by itself represent carried work energy or prove a native actor consumed it. The portable household/labor module introduces that missing lifecycle boundary without changing persistent forecast schemas yet. It is not wired into candidate selection or native execution.

Each finite job identifies a paid request, action kind, pickup frame, outbound/return travel quote, number of work opportunities and requested load. The worker port supplies an authoritative paid birth plus unboosted body. Birth cost and activation must match that body; missing, inactive, CARRY-less or WORK-less workers cannot withdraw. Lifetimes reuse spawnedActiveTicks, including CLAIM bodies. The model assumes an intact unboosted body and a fixed route quote; changing damage/boosts/routes still needs refusal/requotation at integration.

Withdrawal moves endpoint energy into cargo. Travel occupies the actor without earning income. Work consumes cargo through a separate settled-consumption port; zero means a rejected/unavailable work opportunity, never successful progress. Full unboosted energy limits per WORK per opportunity are build5, repair1, upgrade1, matching pinned processor sources under tools/processors/node_modules/@screeps/engine/src/processor/intents/creeps. Target completion, controller limits and eligibility belong to the consumption adapter, which must return the actual accepted energy, not the offered maximum. No work-progress or source-income credit is inferred here.

The first work opportunity is pickup+1+outboundTicks. The last opportunity or exhausted cargo starts return; deposit starts after another action frame plus returnTicks. Partial deposits retry. A completed return cannot also authorize another pickup by the same body on that frame. Overlapping jobs miss rather than double-booking a worker. Expiry runs before work/deposit and reports actual remaining cargo loss. Conservation per job is picked=cargo+spent+returned+lost. All results and inputs are detached; jobs/frames are bounded. Ports are synchronous trusted effects: exceptions propagate and do not promise rollback of previously accepted effects.

Existing declared fees must not be generated again as work expenses or double-counted against endpoint pickup. Schema compatibility and native/financial evidence must accompany that integration.

## Resumable labor checkpoints

Checkpoint1 carries a canonical input binding, bounded clock, job summaries and detached paid-body snapshots. Restore checks strict root/job fields, binding, body cost/activation/lifetime, capacity/rate, energy conservation, maximum possible work consumption, phase timing and non-overlapping accepted occupancy for each request. Repeated jobs for one request must retain the same paid birth/body. Validation scans the bounded job set rather than replaying elapsed frames. Checkpoints are capped at256KiB and copyPlain applies its additional node/depth budget.

Reading or creating a checkpoint calls no worker, withdrawal, consumption, deposit or loss ports. Reload after pickup resumes from the saved cargo; completed returns and expiry losses are not repeated. Tests reload every normal lifecycle frame and separately cover partial return, expiry and forged overlap. These checks validate bounded summaries, not the provenance of every historical accepted receipt. Parent household/project integration must cross-check saved birth facts against its authoritative spawn ledger and reconcile cargo/spending/returns/losses against financial and endpoint ledgers. This is essential before exposing results to candidate selection. The module's checkpoint is not yet part of a persisted forecast envelope, so no forecast schema migration is claimed by this addition.
